# Story 4.1 Technical Interview — Consolidated Plan

## Scope
Story 4.1 freezes the human-readable MVP environment scope list and the frequent-action list that Stories 4.2–4.10 and Story 5.1 consume, as decided by ADR 0010. It defines no formal inventory schema, identifier grammar, or dependency graph.

## Artifact
The scope list and the frequent-action list live together in a single Markdown document, `docs/mvp-scope.md`, so that later stories have one reviewable reference point. The document has three sections: a Scope list grouped by the seven stable responsibilities (system, hardware, desktop, workflow, development, user, visual), a Frequent-action list, and a short Change control section. Entries use plain tables relating a capability to its current provider and notes, without formal identifiers or a dependency graph. `capabilities/` is not created by this story. [DEC-001]

## Scope criterion
The frozen list holds the minimum needed to use the laptop as the daily development machine: the capabilities the PRD requires plus the indispensable parts of the maintainer's current workflow. It does not aim at parity with the current machine, which would turn the MVP into a moving target and keep Epic 4 open indefinitely. Everything else is recorded in a Post-MVP candidates section outside the frozen scope and enters later through the evolution path of Story 6.5, once MirrorOS is in daily use; the shell and visual polish may also keep evolving after migration. [DEC-010]

## Coverage
The scope list covers the whole ready environment, not only what `configure` applies. Each entry marks its origin, installation (Epic 2) or configure (Epic 4), so Story 4.2 does not reapply installed state and Story 5.1 finds every capability in one place. Installation-delivered capabilities such as hibernation, Btrfs snapshots, network connectivity, or CPU microcode are named at capability level with a reference to their authoritative source (`install/plan/policy.json`, `hardware/rules/`, the relevant ADR) instead of duplicating package lists, which avoids a second source of truth that could drift. Software is named only where it is a visible provider choice. Adding or removing an installation capability is a scope change under change control. [DEC-002]

## Frozen capabilities
The frozen list follows the scope criterion and marks each entry as installation-delivered or applied by `configure`, with either a decided provider or an open provider and its owning story. System covers the base system and boot, storage with snapshots and rollback, zram and hibernation, NetworkManager networking, time, locale and console keyboard, and sudo, all from installation, plus audio and Bluetooth (open, Story 4.6) and package updates with AUR review through yay (Story 4.3). Hardware covers microcode, graphics, and VM guest support from the hardware rules, plus brightness, media keys, battery, lid, and suspend (open, Story 4.6). Desktop covers the Hyprland compositor, the SDDM and uwsm login, and the owned Quickshell shell for bar, launcher, notifications, OSD, and session menu, all decided, plus screen lock and idle (open, Story 4.7), the polkit agent, portals, clipboard with history, and screenshots (open, Story 4.6), and keyboard shortcuts in Hyprland's native configuration (Story 4.7); lock and polkit are never owned QML. Workflow covers the terminal, interactive shell, and file manager (open, Story 4.5, with yazi and Nautilus as file-manager candidates), tmux (decided), and the browser (open, Story 4.9). Development covers Git, Neovim and Pi with external configuration, and Docker as container runtime, all decided and implemented in Story 4.8, plus CLI search utilities and the Node.js runtime (open, Story 4.8). User covers the installed account, user configuration linked from external repositories, and secrets and private state that always stay outside MirrorOS. Visual covers typography, a coherent color palette, and cursor and icons (open, Story 4.10), plus the owned SDDM theme. Provider suggestions made during the interview for open entries are indicative only. [DEC-011]

Laptop-specific capabilities such as firmware and BIOS updates through fwupd, the fingerprint reader, and other Framework adjustments are neither frozen nor post-MVP: they are recorded as pending target-laptop availability and decided through hardware adaptation once the laptop can be validated. Post-MVP candidates outside the frozen scope include the Plymouth, LUKS, and autologin bundle, tmuxinator, atuin, lazygit, gh and gcloud, and applications such as calibre, GIMP, and Insomnia. [DEC-011, DEC-006, DEC-010]

## Frequent actions
The frequent-action list is a baseline to start from, explicitly not set in stone. It covers session and power (lock, log out, suspend, hibernate, reboot or power off), launching (launcher, terminal, browser, file manager), windows and workspaces (close, directional focus and move, resize and floating, fullscreen, go to and move to a workspace, previous, next, and back-and-forth workspace, pin, grouping or tabbed and split orientation, moving a workspace to another monitor, switching monitor layout, and the dropdown terminal scratchpad), desktop operations (region and full-screen capture, copy and paste across terminal and graphical applications, clipboard history, dismissing and reviewing notifications), hardware controls (volume, brightness, media), connectivity (Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, through the shell or a TUI), starting or reattaching a tmux session, and showing the keyboard-shortcut reference. Hyprland does not limit which actions are possible; it shapes the bindings, which belong to Story 4.7. Until Story 4.7 completes, the list is adjusted as a reviewed update fed by VM exploration; afterwards a change is an explicit scope change, so NFR48 and Story 5.1 have a stable target. [DEC-012]

Story 4.7 takes the maintainer's current i3 configuration as the primary source of muscle memory and translates it to Hyprland equivalents, using Omarchy and the community as secondary sources and documenting unavoidable differences such as the i3 tree model versus Hyprland's dwindle and master layouts. Bindings inside tmux, Neovim, and Pi remain owned by their external repositories and are not part of this list. [DEC-012]

Keyboard remapping with kanata or kmonad is a Post-MVP candidate: it is not in current use, and the maintainer's daily Ferris Sweep keyboard remaps in its own firmware. If the Framework's built-in keyboard needs home-row mods or Miryoku, remapping enters through the evolution path of Story 6.5. [DEC-013]

## Compositor provider
Hyprland is the provisional compositor provider in the frozen scope list; Niri leaves the MVP scope and remains an uncommitted future trial. The capability itself remains the Wayland compositor and graphical session, and the provider stays replaceable beneath the compositor provider boundary. Because the PRD, product brief, architecture, and epics name Niri, the change is recorded in a new ADR 0011 delivered with this plan; no code depends on Niri, so only documentation is affected. Provider choices that depend on the compositor, such as portal, bar, launcher, lock, and shortcuts, are resolved against Hyprland. [DEC-003]

Following the Epic 3 precedent, ADR 0011 (Accepted) prevails over the Niri mentions in the PRD and product brief, which stay unchanged as the record of the original product intent; the ADR states this divergence explicitly. `docs/architecture.md` is amended to name Hyprland with a reference to ADR 0011, the reserved tree path becomes `desktop/compositor/providers/hyprland/`, and Hyprland replaces Niri as the example of tool-owned Lua configuration, since Hyprland is configured in Lua from version 0.55. `docs/epics.md` is amended in Stories 4.1 and 4.4 and in the Additional Requirements line on the compositor provider, while the PRD-derived NFR33 copy keeps its wording. Research, brainstorming, and earlier interview records are not changed. [DEC-004]

## Session start
The graphical session starts through SDDM, whose greeter runs on Hyprland with a small MirrorOS-owned QML theme, so the target laptop (Framework 13 Pro) presents a coherent, premium login rather than a text greeter. The session is always launched through `uwsm start hyprland.desktop`, which keeps systemd session integration consistent and avoids the logout failure Omarchy hit when its display manager bypassed uwsm. Login requires the password set during installation and there is no autologin, because the root filesystem is unencrypted and no first-boot provisioning needs it. The theme is a small owned asset, not a theming framework. Lock, logout, and compositor switching are handled inside the session and by session entries, independently of the display manager; if the greeter fails on graphics, another TTY remains available for diagnosis. greetd with tuigreet and greetd with ReGreet were rejected. [DEC-005]

## Desktop shell
The desktop shell is a MirrorOS-owned, minimal Quickshell shell bounded to the MVP capabilities, giving the maintainer control over a coherent premium experience. Omarchy is the design reference rather than a dependency: its patterns, structure, and appearance guide the work, and specific fragments may be copied under its MIT license with attribution in `docs/attribution.md`, but its roughly 45,000-line shell is neither forked nor vendored, and Omarchy is not used as-is. Because QML is not in the architecture's language table and owned code needs a documented recurring need under NFR37, ADR 0012 admits QML for the shell, records that justification, and excludes control-panel, welcome, and background-management functionality; it is delivered with this plan together with the architecture amendment. [DEC-007, TODO-003]

Which capabilities the shell absorbs and which stay in dedicated tools is not frozen by this story. As a guideline for exploration, the shell covers visible surfaces (bar, launcher, notifications, OSD, session menu, clipboard-history picker, wallpaper) while hyprlock, hypridle, hyprpolkitagent, wl-clipboard with cliphist, and grim with slurp remain dedicated tools. The only frozen principle is that authentication, meaning the screen lock and the polkit agent, is never project-owned QML. [DEC-008, TODO-004]

## Incremental orchestration
Story 4.1 freezes what the environment must do, not every provider. It freezes the capabilities of the seven responsibilities, the frequent-action list at action level (key bindings belong to Story 4.7), and the providers already decided: Hyprland, SDDM with uwsm, and the owned Quickshell shell. Every undecided provider is marked as open together with the story that will select it. Selecting a provider for an open entry is a reviewed update of `docs/mvp-scope.md`, while adding or removing a capability is a scope change. The Story 4.1 acceptance criterion in `docs/epics.md` is amended accordingly, from naming every piece of software to naming every capability, the providers already selected, and the owning story of each open choice. [DEC-008]

Provider selection follows two loops. Exploration happens by hand in a personal persistent VM installed from a MirrorOS ISO, reverting to a clean-install snapshot as needed; it stays outside the product and nothing from it is committed. Registration happens when a choice proves right: the owning story's plan turns it into `configure` scripts and native configuration, a clean installation plus `configure` in the VM proves it without manual steps, and `docs/mvp-scope.md` is updated under change control. [DEC-009]

MirrorOS adds no exploration-VM tooling or procedure for the MVP. Exploration uses the maintainer's existing private laboratory at `~/qemu/mirroros-lab/`, which already provides restorable checkpoints, VirGL graphics, and SSH access, and which stays local workflow tooling outside the product and source control. Exploring Hyprland and SDDM requires its `--gl` mode, and because the laboratory has no audio device, audio exploration for Story 4.6 needs a laboratory improvement or validation on the laptop. In-product automation is reconsidered only if repeated use justifies it. [DEC-014]

Change control relies on git alone. The Change control section of `docs/mvp-scope.md` states the classification between reviewed updates and scope changes; every change is a commit that cites the owning story, and a scope change also cites its justification, which is the story, an ADR when a normative decision such as the compositor or encryption is affected, or the evolution path of Story 6.5. The document keeps no change history of its own, and ordinary scope changes do not need an ADR. [DEC-015]

## Deferred candidates
Plymouth is outside the frozen MVP scope, so the premium appearance starts at the SDDM greeter. In Omarchy, Plymouth earns its place by hosting the LUKS password prompt; with MirrorOS's unencrypted root it would only be a brief splash, while still requiring changes to the installation-owned boot chain. Plymouth, optional LUKS encryption, and autologin with LUKS are therefore recorded together as one future candidate that the maintainer expects to adopt. It requires a new ADR amending ADR 0003, would be owned by installation, and would need only a bounded validation (automated tests, one boot per bootloader, one hibernate/resume cycle, one Limine snapshot boot) rather than a full Epic 2 revalidation. Until then the login keeps a password and no autologin, and the SDDM theme is designed so it can later match a Plymouth theme. `docs/mvp-scope.md` lists the candidate in a short Deferred candidates section outside the frozen scope. [DEC-006, DEC-005]

## Deliverables and alignment
The plan delivers a new `docs/mvp-scope.md` with the scope list per responsibility (origin and decided or open provider with its owning story), the baseline frequent-action list, change control, items pending target-laptop availability, and deferred and post-MVP candidates; it names no personal repositories, personal i3 configuration, or private laboratory, leaving the identity of external repositories to Stories 4.3 and 4.8. ADR 0011 records Hyprland as the provisional compositor provider and prevails over the Niri mentions in the PRD and product brief. ADR 0012 records the owned Quickshell shell, admits QML as a project-owned language bounded to that shell, documents the NFR37 justification, excludes control-panel, welcome, and background-management functionality, keeps authentication out of owned QML, and names Omarchy as design reference. `docs/architecture.md` is amended for Hyprland, for QML in the language table and selection rules, to link the scope document, and to narrow the remaining future-enhancement item to keyboard bindings in Story 4.7, without deciding where the shell lives under `capabilities/`. `docs/epics.md` is amended in Story 4.1, Story 4.4, and the Additional Requirements compositor line. Attribution, the PRD, the product brief, research, `operations/`, and tests stay unchanged because no Omarchy code is copied and nothing executable changes. [DEC-016, DEC-004, DEC-008]

No new automated test is added, since a validator for a Markdown scope document would be custom code without a recurring need. Verification relies on commands: a Niri search over the architecture and epics, a heading comparison of the new ADRs against the template, the FR coverage loop, the untouched `configure` contract tests, `git diff --check`, and a gitleaks scan. The interview artifacts are committed with the change. [DEC-016]
