# Epic 4 Restructure Technical Interview — Consolidated Plan

## Problem
After Story 4.1, exploration of the daily environment is already free: it happens by hand in the maintainer's private VM laboratory and nothing from it is committed. The rigidity lies in how registration is sliced. Stories 4.2 and 4.3 are horizontal foundations that would build `configure` machinery and provenance records before any real capability is applied, which strains NFR37. Stories 4.4–4.10 are cut by component while real work crosses them: Story 4.6 bundles unrelated capabilities, the owned Quickshell shell is spread across Stories 4.6, 4.7, and 4.10, and visual coherence touches every component but comes last. Their acceptance criteria anticipate decisions that VM exploration should inform. [DEC-001]

## Scope of this plan
This plan restructures Epic 4 as documentation only. It rewrites the Epic 4 story breakdown in `docs/epics.md` and aligns the owning-story references in `docs/mvp-scope.md` and, if needed, `docs/architecture.md`. Implementing the first story of the restructured epic belongs to a later plan, so that the shape of implementation is not fixed before evidence exists, following the documentation-only precedent of plan 02-12. [DEC-001]

## Epic shape
The restructured Epic 4 follows a walking skeleton followed by vertical slices and a closure story. Story 4.2 is the walking skeleton: a minimal `configure` that applies a first usable slice end to end. Intermediate stories are predefined vertical slices, each a usable experience with outcome-level acceptance criteria rather than tool-level ones, so that FR traceability and the remaining MVP work stay visible in `docs/epics.md`. Their order, grouping, and boundaries are not fixed: they may be reordered, split, or merged as a reviewed update of `docs/epics.md` when VM exploration shows a better cut. Only the walking skeleton comes first and the closure story comes last. [DEC-002]

The former horizontal foundations do not survive as separate stories. A common "registration done" contract applies to every slice, so the `configure` mechanism, provenance, and postconditions grow with each slice instead of being built in advance. This avoids building machinery without a consumer and keeps each increment independently provable in the VM. [DEC-002]

## Registration contract
The obligations of the former Stories 4.2 and 4.3 are distributed in three tiers so that machinery appears only when a slice consumes it. [DEC-003]

The common per-slice contract is stated once in the Epic 4 introduction and cited by every slice, and it applies only to what each slice applies. A slice is registered when its capabilities are applied by bounded Bash `configure` scripts over native formats (ADR 0010); every package and external artifact it adds carries provenance with name, source class, repository or URL, identity evidence where the source offers it, and review responsibility; AUR packages have a reviewed `PKGBUILD` and are built unprivileged; external configuration repositories are checked out or linked rather than copied; privilege is limited to the operations that need it and user configuration does not run as root; `configure` checks the slice's postconditions; a full rerun on an already configured system makes no unnecessary mutation; a clean installation plus `configure` in the reference VM succeeds without manual steps; `docs/mvp-scope.md` records the selected providers; and no secret or personal content is committed. [DEC-003]

Story 4.2 establishes once the mechanism properties that later slices inherit: `operations/configure` as an independently invocable entry point, prerequisite validation before any mutation, a failure outcome that identifies the failed step and is never presented as a ready environment, safe rerun after interruption, and secret-free evidence. [DEC-003]

Two clauses are conditional and are implemented by the first slice that needs them rather than in advance, under NFR37: runtime secret supply through a protected prompt, descriptor, or permission-restricted temporary file with an actionable blocked outcome when an external secret is missing, and detection of two owners of the same package, file, or service. The obligations inherited from ADRs 0003, 0008, and 0009 are outside the contract and receive an owner separately. [DEC-003]

## Walking skeleton
Story 4.2 delivers the thinnest slice that still crosses the riskiest path end to end: a minimal usable graphical login. It applies SDDM with its stock theme, keeping password login and no autologin; a session always started through `uwsm start hyprland.desktop`; Hyprland with minimal native configuration to open a terminal, close a window, and exit the session; and a terminal emulator, interactive shell, and tmux. This is the smallest state the maintainer can live in and iterate from, and it validates ADR 0011 and the Story 4.1 session decisions early, while a TTY-only skeleton would leave the graphical risk untested. [DEC-004]

Story 4.2 selects the open terminal and interactive-shell providers from VM exploration, as a reviewed update of `docs/mvp-scope.md`. The file manager, the owned SDDM theme, and the owned Quickshell shell are deliberately excluded so the first slice ships stable and the uncertain owned QML work is iterated later. [DEC-004]

Story 4.2 also defines the two execution contexts of `configure` and integrates the installer invocation. `configure` runs either against the running installed system, for independent reruns and VM iteration, or against a target root, invoked by the guided installer after base installation and before the first normal reboot as required by Epic 3 DEC-015 and DEC-017. The contexts differ sharply: the installer operates through `arch-chroot`, where systemd units can be enabled but not started, user configuration needs `runuser` or an equivalent, and unprivileged AUR builds happen inside the target. Surfacing this architectural risk in the skeleton prevents later slices from assuming a live system and breaking in the chroot at closure. The common per-slice contract therefore requires every slice to work in both contexts. The maintainer's exploration loop is unaffected: `configure` runs against the installed VM with snapshots, and clean installation is reserved for registration proof. [DEC-005, DEC-003]

## Inherited ADR obligations
The obligations that ADRs 0003, 0008, and 0009 route to the former Story 3.2 belong to a dedicated vertical slice, "Safe system updates", whose user outcome is updating the system, AUR packages included, with review and without breaking boot. It owns `yay` as the installed-system AUR path with reviewed `PKGBUILD` and unprivileged build, the pinned and reviewed post-installation update path of the two Limine snapshot tools on which the default `btrfs-limine` option depends, and the response to Gradle dependencies pinned by version only. Keeping it separate avoids fattening the skeleton and avoids pushing a default-option concern to the closure story. The general reviewed, unprivileged AUR build mechanics remain in the common contract and are applied by whichever slice first installs from the AUR. Accepted ADRs keep saying "Story 3.2"; the retired Epic 3 correspondence table redirects it to this slice. [DEC-006, DEC-003]

## Slices
After Story 4.1, Epic 4 has seven stories. Their order is indicative and the intermediate ones remain reorderable, splittable, and mergeable under DEC-002. [DEC-007, DEC-002]

Story 4.2 is the minimal graphical login skeleton. Story 4.3, desktop shell and session control, delivers the owned Quickshell bar, launcher, notifications, OSD, and session menu, the screen lock and idle behavior, window, workspace, and monitor bindings, the dropdown scratchpad, and the shortcut reference; it stands alone because owned QML is the most uncertain and iteration-heavy work. Story 4.4, hardware and connectivity, delivers audio, Bluetooth, brightness, media keys, battery, lid, suspend and hibernate in daily use, and Wi-Fi and Bluetooth connection through the shell or a terminal interface; it is only partly validatable in the VM, which lacks an audio device. Story 4.5, desktop integration and graphical applications, delivers portals, the polkit agent, clipboard with history, screenshots, the file manager, and the browser; the browser lives here because its acceptance depends on portals, file selection, and clipboard. Story 4.6, development tooling, delivers Git, Neovim and Pi from their external repositories, Docker, Node.js, and command-line search utilities. Story 4.7 is safe system updates. Story 4.8, visual coherence and first-login closure, delivers the owned SDDM theme, typography, palette, cursor and icons, checks that the whole frozen scope is covered, and confirms that first login opens without a welcome application or background daemon. [DEC-007, DEC-004, DEC-006]

## Keyboard bindings and frequent actions
Story 4.3 owns the binding conventions: the modifier scheme and the translation from the maintainer's i3 configuration established by Story 4.1, the shortcut reference, and the absence of conflicting bindings. Every slice binds its own frequent actions under those conventions and adds them to the reference, so a slice is not registered while one of its frequent actions lacks a keyboard path. Story 4.2 carries only the minimal bindings of the skeleton, and Story 4.3 may adjust them once the conventions exist; this rework is an accepted cost. [DEC-008, DEC-004]

The frequent-action list stays adjustable as a reviewed update until Story 4.8 completes; afterwards a change is a scope change. Freezing after Story 4.3 was rejected as premature because hardware and desktop actions would not yet be explored in the VM. Story 4.8 verifies FR64, a documented keyboard path for every frequent action. This plan updates `docs/mvp-scope.md` to move the freeze point from former Story 4.7 to Story 4.8 and to renumber open-provider owners according to the new slices. [DEC-008, DEC-007]

## Requirement mapping
Cross-cutting FRs are mapped to the story that establishes them, and the common contract makes later slices keep them. Story 4.1 keeps FR20, FR62, and FR63. Story 4.2 establishes FR22, FR23, FR24, FR25, FR26, FR27, FR28, FR30, FR31, FR61, and FR65 (terminal). Story 4.3 covers FR23, FR64, and FR65 (launcher, bar, notifications). Story 4.4 covers FR23 and FR31. Story 4.5 covers FR23, FR31, and FR65 (browser, portals, clipboard, screenshots). Story 4.6 covers FR21, FR23, and FR29. Story 4.7 covers FR21 and NFR12. Story 4.8 closes FR30, FR31, FR64, and FR65. [DEC-009]

FR21 is also applied by Story 4.2 through the contract but is mapped to Stories 4.6 and 4.7, where external repositories and AUR provenance carry the real weight. FR29 is mapped indicatively to Story 4.6 as the likely first consumer of secrets; because secret handling is a conditional clause, the mapping moves as a reviewed update if another slice needs it first. FR24 and FR61 keep their PRD wording about capability modules; following the 02-12 precedent, ADR 0010 prevails and they are satisfied by per-slice scripts with declared prerequisites. [DEC-009, DEC-003]

## Numbering and ordering
Story numbers are stable identifiers rather than positions, because `docs/mvp-scope.md`, the FR Coverage Map, plans, summaries, and commits reference them; renumbering was rejected for the same reason Epic 3 preserved its numbers. The Epic 4 introduction states that the order is indicative: Story 4.2 comes first, Story 4.8 comes last, and the others are ordered by VM evidence without renumbering. Splitting a story adds new numbers after the highest existing one while Story 4.8 remains the closure story, and merging retires one number and leaves a "Merged into 4.x" note in its place, as the retired Epic 3 section does. Every reorder, split, or merge is a commit citing its evidence. [DEC-010, DEC-002]

## References to former numbering
Accepted ADRs are not modified, following the precedent set for former Story 3.2. Because numbers 4.3–4.8 are reused with new meanings, `docs/epics.md` adds a correspondence table from the former Epic 4 numbering to the new one, placed before the stories so that readers arriving from ADRs 0010, 0011, and 0012 can translate: former 4.2 maps to 4.2 plus the common contract; 4.3 to the common contract plus 4.6 and 4.7; 4.4 to 4.2; 4.5 to 4.2 for terminal, shell, and tmux and to 4.5 for the file manager; 4.6 to 4.4 and 4.5; 4.7 to 4.3; 4.8 to 4.6; 4.9 to 4.5; and 4.10 to 4.8. `docs/mvp-scope.md`, `docs/architecture.md`, and the retired Epic 3 section of `docs/epics.md` are updated to the new numbering, with former Story 3.2 redirected to Story 4.7. `operations/configure` and `docs/procedures/configure.md` still correctly cite Story 4.2 and stay unchanged. [DEC-011, DEC-006, DEC-008]
